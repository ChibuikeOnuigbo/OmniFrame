# 100-video stress test — 26 November 2026

Created 100 video assets using distinct IDs/blob URLs, added 100 one-second
clips via editor actions, applied blur to every clip and added 100 Color Hold
instances. Assets reuse one authorized eight-second Pexels fixture: this tests
project/resource count, not 100 distinct movie files or import-dialog throughput.
Sought through all 100 clips and checked actual decoder readiness. Then played
sequential clips and ten overlapping video tracks with blur; Color Hold retains
its original first-second range on those extended layered clips.

| Measurement | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Retained video elements after visiting 100 assets | 107 | 24 |
| Playback cache entries | unbounded by policy | 16 |
| Seek wait p50 | 84 ms | 100 ms |
| Seek wait p95 | 519 ms | 599 ms |
| Setup actions | 203 ms | 231 ms |

The fix retains up to 16 cached playback videos and 16 audio elements, evicts
oldest inactive entries, unloads their sources and disconnects audio nodes.
Every element needed by the current frame is protected, even when active layers
exceed the cache target. Thumbnail sampling has a separate eight-element pool.
The initial assertions mistakenly counted that pool; corrected selectors identify
playback entries explicitly. No decoder-dependent layer is silently dropped.

Final sequential test: 87 compositor submissions in 3.10 seconds. Ten-layer
sample: 51 submissions in 1.51 seconds. These are submissions, not verified
unique presented frames. Source was reused, layers are opaque, preview was Low,
and Chromium uses cloud/software rendering. Before/after timings are noisy and
do not show a seek-speed improvement. 100 simultaneous video decoders were not
tested. No claim of lag-free 4K or competitor superiority follows.
